## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Culture-first creative strategy for hospitality, food and beverage, retail, and sports brands: campaign ideas, partnership concepts, event activations, and the decks that sell them. [I]

**What it does:** Chip turns a brand's existing assets (heritage, a signature product, a neighborhood, a calendar moment) into a concrete, pitch-ready idea: a launch campaign, a partnership or collab, an event activation, a merch or retail extension. He delivers it as a strategic presentation with a rollout plan, partner suggestions, and a commercial framework, then supports creative direction through execution. [I]

**Product category (how buyers search):** creative strategist, brand strategist, creative consultant, experiential marketing, event activation, brand partnerships, pitch deck design. Site headline: "Cultural Partnerships & Brand Strategy." [V]

**Product type:** Independent creative consultancy / freelance service, since April 2017. [V]

**Business model:** Project fees, scoped up front. Every engagement starts with a written scope and a fixed project fee. No hourly billing for core work. [V] Specific prices are not published here; ask Chip.

2. **Small multi-unit F&B and hospitality groups** (2–15 locations) opening, expanding, or relaunching.
3. **Heritage hospitality brands** with an anniversary, landmark, or signature product.
4. **Specialty and lifestyle retail**, including streetwear, coffee-retail hybrids, specialty markets, and cannabis retail.
5. **Sports and culture properties** (local teams, leagues and venues) for partnership and fan-engagement concepts.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Independent and growing hospitality brands have real cultural equity but no one whose job is to turn it into ideas, partnerships, and moments that drive visits and revenue. [I]

**Why alternatives fall short:** [I]
- Agencies are priced and staffed for larger retainers and often deliver generic campaigns.
- In-house teams or owners are consumed by operations, so marketing becomes posting specials and discounts.
- Generic freelancers execute assets (posts, flyers) but don't originate strategy or partnerships.
- Doing nothing means openings and anniversaries pass as ordinary days.

**What it costs them:** a once-only moment wasted; discounting that trains customers to wait; neighbors and press never told a story worth repeating. [I]

**Emotional tension:** fear of looking corporate or try-hard in a neighborhood that rejects it; fear of spending on an idea the team can't execute. [I]

## Competitive Landscape

[I. To be refined with the competitor-profiling skill per market.]

**Direct:** Local boutique creative and branding agencies and hospitality marketing firms in each market. They fall short on price and on cultural, idea-first thinking for small operators.
**Secondary:** Social media managers and freelance designers. They execute content but don't originate partnerships, activations, or commercial frameworks. Chip deliberately doesn't compete on execution work.
**Indirect:** The owner doing it themselves; PR firms; doing nothing. These lose the strategic idea and the partner network.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:** [I]
- **Brand extension, not brand invention.** Concepts use only assets the brand already owns.
- **Ideas with a mechanic.** Each concept has a specific hook, such as a one-day exception to a closed day, a stamp passport, or a numbered collectible run. Not a list of event ideas.
- **Operator realism.** Rollout calendars, unit caps, cut-off times, production notes, permits, and gated commercial frameworks with no commitment before costing. Comes from years in high-volume retail operations. [V, resume]
- **Local partner thinking.** Concepts pair the brand with real neighboring businesses and makers.
- **Presentation quality.** The deck itself is the proof of thinking.
- **Fixed, scoped fees.** Written scope and fixed project fee before work starts, no hourly billing. [V]

**Why customers choose Chip (hypothesis to validate):** a senior-level idea and a buyer-ready deck at a freelancer's scale and a fixed price, from someone local who understands the culture. [I]

## Objections

[I. Draft responses, to be refined after real conversations.]

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We don't have a marketing budget." | A fixed, scoped project fee with no hourly surprises, sized to what they need. |
| "We've tried events; they don't pay off." | Design around a mechanic with a cap, a cut-off and a recurring follow-on, and define success metrics together up front. |
| "Who else have you done this for?" | Show portfolio work as examples of thinking, labeled honestly as concepts. Ask Chip before naming any client. |
| "We have an agency or a social person." | Good. Chip originates the idea and the partnerships; their team executes. He doesn't do social management, so he isn't competing with them. |
| "Can you mock up a full concept for free first?" | No. Client-requested spec work is declined. Offer a scoped, paid first step instead. |
| "Not the right time." | Tie it to their next dated moment and work backward from it. |

## Switching Dynamics

- **Push:** last launch or holiday fell flat; discounting isn't working; a new location or anniversary is coming with no plan. [I]
- **Pull:** a specific, fully worked idea for *their* brand, presented as a finished deck, from a local who gets the culture, at a fixed fee. [I]
- **Habit:** "We just post on Instagram and run specials." [I]
- **Anxiety:** cost, staff bandwidth, looking gimmicky, unproven freelancer. Answer with a written scope and fixed fee, operational detail, and honest proof. [I]

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** confident, warm, culturally fluent; editorial rather than corporate. [I]
**Style:** short declarative headlines, concrete detail (dates, addresses, unit counts, ingredients), plain claims with sources. Label any industry stat as "a category signal, not a forecast."
**Personality:** culture-first, builder, local, playful, rigorous. [I]

**Watch-out:** Chip's decks often use the "Not X. Y." reveal construction. The copywriting and ad-creative skills flag this as an AI tell. Use it at most once per piece. [I]

**Creative standards:** strategy before visuals; original, distinctive, explainable ideas; real-world constraints respected; commercial value stated; risks and measurement named.

## Guardrails

These apply to every skill and every output that uses this file.

1. **No client names.** Don't name any brand as a client unless Chip confirms it for that specific use. Treat every portfolio brand as a concept or proposal.
2. **Concept vs. commissioned language.** Concepts: "a concept I developed for," "a proposal prepared for," "an unsolicited concept." Commissioned (confirmed only): "work for," "engaged by." Never "worked with," "partnered with," or a logo wall for concepts.
3. **No invented results.** Use only the verified metrics above, worded as in the source ("contributed to"). Never attribute employer results to the consultancy. Never project outcomes for a prospect as promises; label any model "illustrative."
4. **No invented experience or relationships.** Use "13+ years." Las Vegas is the home base; for Indianapolis, say "roots," not residence. No Phoenix local claim. No press, awards, or relationships that aren't verified.
5. **No prices.** This file contains no pricing. Never quote or estimate a fee; ask Chip.
6. **Scope discipline.** Never pitch the work types in "Do not pitch." Never offer free spec work on request.
7. **Sourced claims only.** Industry stats need a named source and a "category signal, not a forecast" framing.
8. **Prospect facts are researched, not assumed.** Every fact about a prospect (opening date, owner name, anniversary, menu item) needs a public source link captured during research. Mark guesses as hypotheses.
9. **Contacts.** Use names and roles found in public sources only. Never guess or pattern-generate email addresses. Don't pass anything off as a personal relationship that doesn't exist.
10. **Partner names in pitches** are suggestions, not commitments. Say "proposed partner" unless the partner has agreed.
11. **Gaps go to Chip.** If a task needs something this file doesn't state, ask instead of assuming.
