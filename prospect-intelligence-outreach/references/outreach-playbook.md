# Outreach Playbook

Formats, structure, follow-ups, and the checklist every draft must pass.

---

## Formats

| Format | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold email** | 120 words or fewer (body) | Default when a published email exists |
| **Contact form or DM** | 60 words or fewer | When only a form, inquiry page, or social inbox is available |
| **LinkedIn connection note** | 300 characters or fewer | When a named decision-maker has a public profile |
| **Follow-up 1** | 50–70 words | 5–7 business days after no reply, adding one new fact or angle |
| **Follow-up 2 (optional)** | 40 words or fewer | 10–14 days later, a short close-the-loop note |

Stop after two follow-ups. A "no" or an opt-out ends the sequence.

**Provisional vs. send-ready drafts.** A draft for a Pursue prospect that isn't Ready to Contact is **provisional**. Its first line, in bold above the draft, reads **Do not send until a written route is confirmed.** A draft becomes **send-ready** only when both are true: the original-source check is Completed for every quote, date, number, award, and plan in it, and the user has a usable written route (Direct or General written). Mark it "Send-ready, checked YYYY-MM-DD." A phone call or visit is only for getting the right written route. Don't pitch the idea unexpectedly on that call. Share it only if the person asks.

## Structure (cold email)

1. **Subject:** specific to their moment, 3–7 words, no clickbait. Example shape: "[Their milestone] and [their owned asset]."
2. **Opener:** who you are in one line, and where you're based if it's relevant and true.
3. **Hook:** one Observed fact about their moment, in their own terms ("By your own count, [business] turns 50 next spring.").
4. **The idea:** one or two sentences naming the mechanic. Enough to show thinking, not a full concept.
5. **Credibility:** one verified line from the context file's proof points. A concept may be cited only if labeled as a concept and cleared.
6. **The ask:** one low-friction ask, such as "Open to a 20-minute call in the next two weeks?"
7. **An out:** optional, one line that respects their plans ("If you already have this covered, I'd still like to hear how you're approaching it.").
8. **Sign-off:** name, studio, website, a valid postal address, and an opt-out line.

**The postal address in the sign-off.** Commercial email has to include a valid physical postal address. Don't use the user's home address by default. Use a business mailing address instead, such as a studio or office address, a P.O. box registered with the postal service, or a private mailbox registered with a commercial mail receiving agency. If the user hasn't given one, leave the placeholder `[business mailing address]` and add it to the still-to-verify list. Before anything is sent, the user should check the rules that apply to the sender and the recipient's location (CAN-SPAM in the US, and any state or international rules). This playbook is guidance, not legal advice.

## Quality Checklist (every draft)

**Truth**
- [ ] Every fact about the prospect is Observed, with its source in the brief
- [ ] Every quote, date, number, and award is marked "verified against original" in the brief (see [evidence-standards.md](evidence-standards.md))
- [ ] Inferred dates are attributed to their source ("by your own count"), and unconfirmed plans are phrased as reported
- [ ] Nothing comes from a research step marked Skipped or Unavailable
- [ ] No Hypothesis is stated as fact
- [ ] No invented familiarity ("I've been a regular for years") unless the user confirms it

**Guardrails from the context file**
- [ ] No client names unless cleared for public use
- [ ] No prices or fee ranges
- [ ] No ROI, revenue, or traffic promises
- [ ] No do-not-pitch services offered
- [ ] No full concept, deck, or mockup offered for free
- [ ] Experience and location claims match the context file exactly
- [ ] Portfolio concepts, if mentioned, are labeled as concepts and cleared

**Craft**
- [ ] 120 words or fewer (email) or 60 or fewer (form/DM)
- [ ] One idea, one ask
- [ ] Reads like a peer, not a vendor; no "I hope this finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "synergy," "leverage"
- [ ] Passes the AI-tell check in `copywriting/references/ai-tells.md` (no "it's not X, it's Y," no "no X, no Y, no Z" lists, no trailing comma clauses)
- [ ] Specific enough that it couldn't be sent to a different business unchanged

## Follow-Up Shapes

**Follow-up 1:** add one new, Observed thing (a recent post, a date confirmation, a press mention) and restate the ask in one line.

**Follow-up 2:** a short close: "I'll leave it here. If [moment] planning opens up, I'd be glad to talk."

---

## When They Reply

| Reply | Next step |
|---|---|
| Interested | Propose two call times. Prepare call notes from the brief, not a deck. Update the ledger to Replied, then Call booked. |
| "Send more info" | One short paragraph and a portfolio link. No custom deck unless the user decides to make one. |
| "Not now" | Thank them, ask when to check back, set a Nurture date in the ledger. |
| "Already have someone" | Thank them; mark Lost with the reason. |
| Asks for a free concept first | Decline politely and offer a paid first step or a call. Never agree to unpaid spec work. |
| Opt-out | Stop immediately and mark Disqualified (opt-out). |
