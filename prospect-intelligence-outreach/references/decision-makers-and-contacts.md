# Decision-Makers and Contact Routes

How to identify who decides, and how to reach them, without inventing anything.

---

## Map Roles to People

Start from the buyer roles in the context file's Personas section. For each prospect, find who holds them:

| Business type | Usually decides | Usually champions |
|---|---|---|
| Independent restaurant, bar, café | Owner, chef-owner, or founding partner | General manager, beverage director |
| Small multi-unit group | Founder or CEO, director of marketing | Brand manager, operations director |
| Heritage or landmark brand | Owner or family principal, president | Marketing director, events manager |
| Specialty or lifestyle retail | Founder, owner | Head of marketing, store or merch lead |
| Resort or hotel outlet | Director of F&B, director of marketing for the property | Outlet GM, events or partnerships lead |
| Sports or culture property | VP or director of partnerships, CMO | Community relations, fan engagement lead |

**Where to find names (public only):** the business's About or Team page, press coverage quoting them, official filings (business or liquor license holders, corporate registrations), award announcements, podcast or event speaker listings, and LinkedIn profiles viewed manually.

**Record for each person:** name, role, source URL, source date, and confidence:
- **High:** named in a role by a Tier 1 source, or by two Tier 2 sources, within 12 months
- **Medium:** one Tier 2 source within 12 months, or a Tier 1 source older than 12 months
- **Low:** an older or single Tier 3 source. Use only with a caveat, and verify before outreach.

## Allowed Contact Routes

In order of preference:

| Route | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Warm introduction** | Through a real mutual connection the user confirms. Never imply one that doesn't exist. |
| **Named-role email published by the business** | For example, an owner or events address on their own site |
| **General business email published by the business** | info@, hello@, events@: address it to the named person |
| **Contact, events, or private-party inquiry form** | Often reaches a decision-maker at small venues. A third-party form counts only if the business's own site links to it. |
| **The business's own social inbox** | Sent manually by the user. Never a personal account. |
| **Phone call to the business's published number** | To ask for the right written route, not to pitch unexpectedly. Share the idea only if the person asks. Never send cold copy by text message. |
| **LinkedIn message** | To a public profile, sent manually by the user. No automation. |
| **In person** | For the home market, a visit at a quiet hour is legitimate. Never pretend to be a customer with a complaint or a vendor with an order. |

**Never:**
- Guess or pattern-generate email addresses (firstname@domain, first.last@domain)
- "Verify" an address by sending to it, or with tools that ping mail servers without the user's say-so
- Use personal email addresses or personal phone numbers not published for business use
- Pull contacts from breached, leaked, or unprovenanced lists
- Scrape LinkedIn, Google Maps, or any site whose terms forbid it
- Bypass logins, CAPTCHAs, or bot protection
- Decode obfuscated email addresses with tools. An address shown to visitors but hidden from scrapers counts as a published General written route. The next action is for the user to view it on the site, and the prospect isn't Ready to Contact until they do.
- Treat a press or PR contact as a route to the decision-maker. Note it, but don't score it.

## Scoring Access

**Access matrix** (kept identical in `SKILL.md`, `references/scoring-rubric.md` and `references/decision-makers-and-contacts.md`)

Person, the best one found:
- **A:** a named decision-maker, high confidence
- **B:** a named decision-maker, medium confidence, or a named champion (GM, marketing, events or partnerships lead)
- **C:** no one named, or only a low-confidence name

Route, the best one published:
- **Direct written:** a confirmed warm introduction, or an email published for that named person or their role
- **General written:** a general business inbox (info@, hello@), a contact or inquiry form on the business's own site (including a third-party form the site itself links to), or the business's own social inbox, used manually
- **Phone or walk-in only:** the business's published phone number, or an in-person visit in a market the user can realistically visit
- **None:** no published route

**Unverified routes.** Score Access only on routes verified in this run. A route that couldn't be found or read scores as None. The prospect stays in the scored ranking on that score. It isn't dropped, and its score isn't raised on assumption.

| Person / Route | Direct written | General written | Phone or walk-in only | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 5 | 4 | 3 | 1 |
| **B** | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| **C** | — | 2 | 2 | 1 |

**Hard gates:** Access 2 or lower can't be Pursue. Access 1 means there is no usable route.

**Pursue vs. Ready to Contact.** Pursue is a tier: 75 or more, with no gate hit. A Pursue prospect is **Ready to Contact** only when its best route is written (Direct or General written) the user can see and use that address or form now, and the original-source check of its outreach claims is complete. A Pursue prospect reachable only by phone or walk-in, or whose published email is obfuscated and not yet viewed by the user, is Pursue but not Ready to Contact. Its required next action is to call or visit (or view the address on the site) to get a written route. A call or visit is for obtaining the route, not for pitching unexpectedly. Any draft for it is provisional and carries the label **Do not send until a written route is confirmed.**

## Compliance Basics (US)

- **CAN-SPAM** applies to commercial email: an accurate sender and subject line, a physical mailing address, and a clear way to opt out. Honor opt-outs promptly.
- Keep the source URL and date for every contact recorded in the ledger.
- Don't target or personalize on sensitive traits (health, finances, religion, politics, sexuality), even if a public post reveals them.
- Outside the US, check local rules before emailing (for example, CASL in Canada and GDPR in the EU are stricter).

This is operational guidance, not legal advice.

## When No One Can Be Named

Say so plainly in the brief. A prospect with no one named is row C of the Access matrix, so Access is 2 at most. Access 2 or lower can't be Pursue, so the prospect stays at Nurture (or lower), and no outreach is drafted or sent. The next action is "find the owner's name" or "find a route." Rescore once a name and a usable route are confirmed.
